CAUSALONTOLOGY 4.0.0 RELEASE PLAN  (v1)

Step-by-step instructions for releasing Causalontology 4.0.0 - the attitude
kind, the predicted_occurrence kind, and the prediction_error kind (the
nineteenth, twentieth, and twenty-first object kinds; the token tier grown from
four to seven), semantics Rules 24 and 25, and the widened assertion
about-reference - to every target language's package ecosystem.

This is a versioned document (the Archive Rule applies on future bumps: a new
version is a new file and the superseded version is moved into archive/ with
git mv). Its line-1 internal header names the document and version and is never
edited on later bumps.

This plan SUBSUMES AND SUPERSEDES the Causalontology 3.0.0 release plan, which
moves to archive/ under its own Archive Rule in the same change that adds this
file. Part One states why: eighteen of the nineteen bindings never implemented
the 3.0.0 delta, so for them the 4.0.0 work folds the 3.0.0 delta in first, and
no separate 3.0.0 release remains planned.

Author: D. R. Dison / ai-university-aiu.

Acronyms are spelled out in full on first use, with the acronym in parentheses,
and the bare acronym thereafter. External proper names are kept verbatim
(SWI-Prolog, Ed25519, Secure Hash Algorithm 256-bit (SHA-256), Git, PyPI, npm,
crates.io, Maven Central, NuGet, RubyGems, Packagist, Hex, LuaRocks, Swift
Package Manager, pub.dev, Go, Zig, Hackage, CPAN, CRAN, vcpkg, Conan, the Julia
General registry).


PART ONE - THE COMMON PRE-RELEASE SECTION (applies to every language)

The single source of truth for 4.0.0 is the frozen schema and conformance suite:
the twenty-one JavaScript Object Notation (JSON) schemas under spec/schema/ and
the one hundred and thirty-seven conformance vectors under conformance/vectors/
(V01-V137). The Python reference binding passes all one hundred and
thirty-seven.

A binding MAY NOT publish 4.0.0 until it passes the 4.0.0 conformance vectors in
its own language. For every binding, in order:

 1. FREEZE. Treat spec/schema/ and conformance/vectors/ as read-only. Do not
    alter a vector to make a binding pass; fix the binding.

 2. IMPLEMENT THE 4.0.0 DELTA. Add, in the binding's own code, the three new
    token-tier kinds, the two new semantic rules, and the widened
    about-reference:
    - the attitude kind (change-order Section B): its schema and its
      identity-bearing fields (holder, attitude_type, content). The holder is a
      MODELED agent inside the ontology (a continuant or token_individual
      identifier), never a cryptographic key; the attitude_type comes from the
      CLOSED enumeration believes, desires, intends, knows, expects, fears
      (extensible only by a future major version); the content is a reference by
      identity to ANY content object INCLUDING another attitude, which is what
      makes nesting (A believes that B believes X) expressible. An attitude
      records what the holder's mind CONTAINS, not what is true - the content
      may be false - and it carries no strength of its own; it is asserted,
      graded, and retracted through the ordinary provenance layer.
    - the predicted_occurrence kind (change-order Section A): its schema and its
      identity-bearing fields (instantiates, interval, predictor, and strength
      when present). It records an occurrence a predictor EXPECTS: instantiates
      names the occurrent type predicted; the interval carries exactly ONE
      temporal dimension (see Rule 24 below); the predictor is a modeled agent
      or construct, not a key; strength is OPTIONAL and identity-bearing when
      present - the predicted probability, part of the predicted content itself,
      a bare number deliberately without the 0..1 bounds of assertion strength.
      A prediction is NOT an assertion that the thing happened: a forecast and a
      report of comparable content mint DIFFERENT identifiers.
    - the prediction_error kind (change-order Section A): its schema and its
      identity-bearing fields (predicted, observed, discrepancy). It pairs a
      predicted_occurrence with the token_occurrence that did or did not fulfil
      it: observed is OPTIONAL, and its absence means the prediction was
      fulfilled by nothing; discrepancy is the signed, graded number read
      actual-minus-expected (positive exceeded expectation, negative fell short,
      zero fulfilled).
    - semantics Rule 24 (A PREDICTION IS NOT A REPORT): a predicted_occurrence
      satisfies no rule that requires a token_occurrence; its interval MUST
      carry exactly one temporal dimension - a wall-clock start (with optional
      end) or an ordinal start_tick (with optional end_tick), never both and
      never neither; a prediction_error MUST reference a predicted_occurrence,
      and when observed is present it MUST instantiate the same occurrent the
      prediction instantiates (the pairing check needs the two referenced
      OBJECTS, so bindings implement it as a helper over the objects, as the
      Python reference does with prediction_pairing_mismatch, rather than
      inside a record-local validator).
    - semantics Rule 25 (AN ATTITUDE'S CONTENT IS QUARANTINED): no reasoning,
      conflict, reachability, or classification rule may treat an attitude's
      content as asserted; content that contradicts the actual record raises no
      conflict (that mismatch IS a false belief, first-class and shareable);
      the holder is forever distinct from the signing source.
    - the widened assertion about-reference: assertion.about now accepts
      attitude, predicted_occurrence, and prediction_error identifiers - and
      cross_stratal_seam identifiers, repairing a 3.0.0 drift (the seam scheme
      had been genuinely absent from the about pattern since 3.0.0).
    - the new conformance vectors V120-V137: group X, prediction and prediction
      error (V120-V127); group Y, attitude and theory of mind (V128-V135);
      group Z, the 4.0.0 identity witnesses (V136 re-pins two frozen 3.0.0
      identifiers byte-for-byte; V137 rejects abbreviated schemes for the new
      kinds, mirroring V107).
    All of it is ADDITIVE and IDENTITY-PRESERVING: content addressing hashes
    only the fields present, so every 3.0.0 record valid under 4.0.0 keeps its
    identifier byte-for-byte (witness V136), and the binding's existing
    identity tests must continue to pass unchanged.

    THE FOLDED-IN 3.0.0 DELTA, STATED HONESTLY. Eighteen of the nineteen
    bindings never implemented the 3.0.0 delta; only the Python reference did.
    For those eighteen, step 2 therefore folds in the 3.0.0 delta FIRST, then
    lays the 4.0.0 delta on top. The 3.0.0 delta, restated compactly from the
    superseded plan:
    - the ordinal tick temporal unit: accept "ticks" as a temporal-window unit;
      order a tick window by integer comparison of tick counts; treat a tick
      window and a wall-clock window as DISJOINT dimensions and refuse to
      convert a tick count to seconds;
    - the cross_stratal_seam kind: its schema, its identity-bearing fields
      (source, target, mechanism_status, chain), and Algorithm F (non-adjacency,
      the drawn-chain rules, the coarsest-stratum home rule, and the
      contradictory-seam and contradictory absent-with-chain checks);
    - the conduit realized_by reference: an optional, identity-bearing,
      scheme-qualified reference field; unbound remains legal.
    A binding at the 2.0.0 level goes straight to 4.0.0; no binding publishes a
    3.0.0. This is why this plan subsumes and supersedes the 3.0.0 release
    plan.

 3. CONFORM. Run the binding's own port of the conformance suite until it
    reports 137/137. Green is the gate; a binding that is not green is PENDING,
    not published. READ THAT NUMBER CORRECTLY, per the measurement recorded in
    Part Three under WHAT 137/137 ACTUALLY COUNTS: it is 137 CHECKS, of which
    38 are driven by the shared vector files V01-V38 and 99 are implemented
    per binding, because the files V39-V137 carry no executable payload. The
    gate is real, but it is not 137 shared data vectors and must not be
    described as such.

 4. BUMP THE VERSION to 4.0.0 in the binding's package manifest.

 5. TAG THE RELEASE in Git (v4.0.3 for the vX.Y.Z channels as of 2026-07-27 -
    Swift, Zig, Packagist, the C++ source tarball, and the LuaRocks
    rockspec's source.tag - or the language-specific tag noted below; Go's is
    bindings/go/v4.0.0 - corrected 2026-07-26: the module lives in the
    major-version subdirectory bindings/go/v4, and for that layout Go drops
    the folder's /v4 suffix from the tag prefix). Tags are added, never moved:
    v4.0.0, v4.0.1 and v4.0.2 stay exactly where they are, because a pushed
    tag is a public, cached surface a consumer may already have pinned.

 6. PUBLISH, then run the post-publish verification: install the PUBLISHED
    4.0.0 package fresh, from the registry, into a clean environment, and run
    its conformance vectors against it. Only a fresh-install green run confirms
    the release.

Signing and provenance are unchanged from 2.0.0: Maven Central artifacts are
GNU Privacy Guard (GPG) signed; see SECURITY.md.

A release is COMPLETE for a language only when its package is published AND a
fresh install passes 137/137. Track it honestly: a language that is not yet
green is listed PENDING with its blocker; a false "all shipped" is not
permitted.

TIGHTENED 2026-07-26, after the packaging defect recorded below. "A fresh
install passes 137/137" now means one specific thing, because the loose
reading is what produced a page of false claims: the ARTIFACT is built, then
installed into a clean location OUTSIDE the checkout, and the suite is run
from a working directory outside the checkout with
CAUSALONTOLOGY_TEST_INSTALLED=1 and CAUSALONTOLOGY_SPEC unset. The runner
prints the binding path and the schema directory it actually read, and aborts
if either is inside the repository. A run that resolves the binding by

TIGHTENED AGAIN 2026-07-27, after the Zig finding. The 2026-07-26 rule was

proof about packaging.

THE PACKAGING DEFECT, AND WHY THE PROOFS ABOVE WERE FALSE (2026-07-26, last
thing that day). Everything published earlier on this date was verified by a
method that could not detect the fault it was meant to detect. Three findings,
plainly, in the order they matter.

  FINDING ONE - THE SCHEMAS WERE NEVER PACKAGED. Every binding except Rust
  located the twenty-one JSON Schemas at a path relative to a REPOSITORY
  CHECKOUT and shipped no copy of its own: Python at
  Path(__file__).parents[3]/"spec"/"schema", JavaScript at
  path.join(__dirname, "..", "..", "spec", "schema"), and the same shape in
  every other language. Inside a checkout that path exists, so every local
  test passed. Inside an installed package it does not exist, so the first
  validate call a real consumer made failed with file-not-found. The
  published PyPI wheel for 4.0.0 contains eleven files and not one schema.

  FINDING TWO - THE CONFORMANCE RUNNERS SHADOWED THE ARTIFACT. Each runner
  reached the binding by relative path - sys.path.insert,
  require("../causalontology.js"), require_relative, import "../lib/..." -
  so a run advertised as a fresh install actually exercised the repository
  source sitting beside it, and read the repository's schemas while doing so.
  Nothing about the package was under test. The Go runner went further and
  set the schema directory to the checkout explicitly.

  FINDING THREE - THEREFORE THE EARLIER PROOFS WERE FALSE, NOT MERELY WEAK.
  A "fresh install 137/137" produced by code that loads the repository is not
  an optimistic reading of a real measurement; it is a number that would have
  come out the same had the package been empty. Every such claim in this plan
  and in PUBLISHING.md for a package registry has been struck or restated.
  Measured honestly: the installed PyPI wheel scores 62/137, a clean Dart
  consumer outside a checkout throws "cannot locate spec/schema" on its first
  validate, and the Go module delivers no spec/schema by go get at all.

  WHAT WAS DONE ABOUT IT. Each binding now carries a byte-for-byte copy of
  the twenty-one schemas inside its own package and prefers that copy over
  the repository path; CAUSALONTOLOGY_SPEC still overrides both. Where the
  package format allows it the schemas are COMPILED IN rather than shipped as
  loose data files - Rust include_str!, Go go:embed, generated source for
  Dart and Kotlin - because a compiled-in copy cannot be dropped by a
  packaging manifest. Every conformance runner gained an installed mode:
  with CAUSALONTOLOGY_TEST_INSTALLED set it resolves the binding the way a
  consumer does, prints the path it loaded and the schema directory it
  actually read, and HARD FAILS if either lies inside the repository, if
  CAUSALONTOLOGY_SPEC is set, or if the bundled copy differs from
  spec/schema by a single byte. The conformance workflow now runs both modes
  for every binding, so a binding that stops shipping its schemas fails the
  build rather than the user. As of this writing all twenty-one vendored or
  compiled-in copies are byte-identical to spec/schema across every binding.

  THE VERSION DECISION (the owner's, recorded here as given). The instruction
  was: ship as 4.0.0 wherever that is physically possible, and 4.0.1 only where
  a registry forbids it. A channel never published at 4.x therefore ships 4.0.0
  correctly the first time: npm, RubyGems, Hex, NuGet, LuaRocks, both Maven
  Central artifacts, CPAN, CRAN, Hackage, and Julia General. pub.dev and the Go
  module proxy are superseded at 4.0.1, because each holds exactly one file per
  version and that file is permanent; on those two, 4.0.0 can never be made to
  serve correct code by any means. PyPI is the exception that proves the rule:
  a PyPI release holds many files, so the broken pair is deleted and the fix is
  uploaded into the SAME 4.0.0 release as a build-tagged wheel, which pip
  prefers on the tie-break. That costs an sdist which can never exist again for
  4.0.0, and it must be done before 2026-08-09, when PyPI stops accepting new
  files on a release older than fourteen days. crates.io
  stays at 4.0.0 because it was always sound, and the v4.0.1 tag channels
  (Swift, Zig, Packagist, the C++ tarball) stay as they are. Package version
  and specification version are therefore allowed to differ - every one of
  these implements specification 4.0.0 - and the 137 vectors are the thing
  that must not differ. The full table is in PUBLISHING.md. Publishing a
  superseding version and yanking or retracting the broken one are two
  separate acts, each needing the owner's separately named go-ahead;
  retraction is not deletion, so the superseding release is the fix and the
  yank is only a signpost.

WHAT 137/137 ACTUALLY COUNTS (measured 2026-07-27, across all nineteen
bindings). The number this release is gated on does not mean what its label
says, so it is recorded here plainly rather than left to be discovered again.

  IT COUNTS CHECKS, NOT VECTOR FILES. "137/137" is 137 hand-written,
  per-language assertions. Of those, 38 are driven by the frozen shared
  vector files - exactly V01-V38 - and the other 99 share no data with any
  other binding at all. Method: strace of openat on every runner, then a
  shadow vectors directory in which the CONTENTS of V39-V137 were replaced by
  "{}" while the filenames stayed intact, then a second shadow with those 99
  files deleted, then a positive control with all 137 gutted to prove the
  method had teeth. Every one of the nineteen runners opens 38 vector files
  for their contents and no more. Rust and Haskell do open all 137, but only
  to lift the label printed on the PASS line: against the gutted-content
  shadow both still print 137/137 and CONFORMANT.

  THE CAUSE IS IN THE VECTOR FILES, NOT ONLY IN THE RUNNERS. All 99 files
  from V39 to V137 carry no executable payload - no "input", no "given", no
  "steps". Their "operation" field reads, literally, "see bindings/*/
  conformance runner". They are named, grouped stubs carrying an "expect"
  block of booleans and a prose note. There is nothing in them to execute, so
  no runner could read them for meaning even if it tried.

  THE HONEST HEADLINE. Not "137/137 vectors". Either "137/137 conformance
  checks passed (38 driven by the frozen shared vectors; 99 implemented per
  binding)" or, if one number is wanted, "38/38 shared data vectors + 99
  per-binding checks". A stricter figure is also defensible: of the 38 files
  that are read, only 26 change any verdict when their bytes are destroyed
  (V01-V19, V21-V25, V34, V35); gutting all 137 leaves 111 checks still
  passing.

  WHAT THIS DOES AND DOES NOT UNDERMINE. It does not mean the bindings are
  unverified: 137 assertions per language really do run, and the
  schema-packaging proofs above are unaffected, since those turn on whether
  an installed artifact can validate at all. What it undermines is the
  specific claim - made in conformance/README.md, in bindings/README.md and
  in the standard's own README - that the shared vector files are what makes
  nineteen independent implementations agree. Today that holds for V01-V38.
  For V39-V137 the nineteen bindings agree only in that nineteen ports were
  each written to the same prose note.

  A SECOND MEASUREMENT THAT BEARS ON EVERY FRESH-INSTALL PROOF: only the Rust
  runner detects a vectors directory with files missing. Delete 99 of the 137
  and Elixir, Go, Swift and Zig still report 137/137 and CONFORMANT; seven
  more bindings happen to crash on a filename lookup, which is an accident
  rather than a guard. Rust alone refuses with an accurate diagnosis, and
  even Rust passes when the files are present but hollow.

  THE CHEAPEST REPAIR, IN ORDER OF COST. (1) Stop printing the word "vectors"
  for a number that counts checks - nineteen one-line changes, and it removes
  the false claim immediately. (2) Port the Rust completeness check into the
  other eighteen runners, roughly ten lines each. (3) Each of the 99 stubs
  ALREADY lists in its "expect" block every assertion the check owes, so have
  each runner require that its check for vector n answers every key in that
  file's "expect" object, comparing the answered key-set to the file's. That
  is one shared helper per language rather than 99 rewrites, and it makes the
  file authoritative over what must be checked - which is what "137 vectors"
  asserts. Writing real payloads into the 99 files is the complete fix, but
  that is a data project of 99 files times 19 runners and must not gate the
  relabel. NONE OF THIS IS DONE. What is recorded above is a measurement, and
  the vectors themselves were not touched.

MATURITY NOTE. The bindings are at different maturity, and this plan says so
plainly. It gives the steps and states each language's PREREQUISITE - a binding
must first implement the folded-in 3.0.0 delta where it is missing, then the
4.0.0 delta, and pass 137/137. It does not claim a language is released until
it is. Where a 2.0.0 binding is already awaiting a human step (the Contributor
License Agreement on the vcpkg and Conan ports for C++, the web-form submission
for the R package on CRAN, the PAUSE upload for Perl, the contested Julia
General registration, the Hackage account and token for Haskell), the 4.0.0
release for that language inherits the same human gate and is PENDING until it
clears - recorded here, not hidden.
